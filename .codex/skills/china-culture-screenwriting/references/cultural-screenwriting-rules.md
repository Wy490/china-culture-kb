# Cultural Screenwriting Rules

## Evidence Boundary

Every generated cultural story should distinguish:

- Fact: present in knowledge-base entry or reliable source.
- Plausible dramatization: fills scene, action, dialogue, emotion, or pacing while respecting known facts.
- Fictional addition: invented character, event, object, relationship, or coincidence for dramatic structure.
- Unknown: content that should remain cautious or be marked for verification.

Do not turn plausible dramatization into historical certainty. Do not relocate real people or events to the user's target city unless the entry supports that connection.

## Scene Standard

A usable scene needs:

- location and time impression
- visible action
- conflict, discovery, or decision
- emotional turn
- cultural object, place, phrase, ritual, craft, or historical pressure
- a reason it advances the story

Weak scene signals:

- only background explanation
- only praise
- only scenic description
- no character choice
- no visual anchor
- no change from beginning to end

## Genre Notes

### character_story

Focus on choice and consequence, not biography. Avoid birth-to-death summaries.

### historical_drama

Anchor in verifiable event pressure. Make conflict legible through documents, office, family, law, ritual, place, or public consequence.

### legend_story

Use symbolic images and oral-story rhythm, but keep the human choice clear. Mark uncertain versions when relevant.

### heritage_promo

Show hands, tools, process, material, apprenticeship, transmission pressure, and why the craft still matters.

### ai_comic_drama

Use strong opening hook, compressed conflict, expressive reactions, dialogue collision, panelable action, and an ending question. Continuity matters more than standalone cleverness.

### children_story

Keep conflict understandable and non-cruel. Use concrete objects, gentle stakes, repeated motifs, and emotional learning.

### documentary_short

Use real site, source trace, present-day observer, and restrained narration. Do not over-fictionalize.

### explainer_video

Use question -> concept -> example -> visual metaphor -> takeaway. Keep one idea per segment.

## Repair Checks

When revising, ask:

- What fact or source is this scene grounded in?
- What changed because this scene happened?
- What can the camera see?
- What does the protagonist want?
- What pressure blocks the protagonist?
- What cultural detail is doing story work rather than decorating?
- Is any fictional addition clearly bounded?
